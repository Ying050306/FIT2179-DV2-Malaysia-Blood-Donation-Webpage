Malaysia's blood banks constantly face the challenge of maintaining optimal reserves. Every hour, a patient needs blood that may not be there. This story traces 6 years of donation records across states to uncover regional supply vulnerabilities, highlighting how an aging donor demographic and uneven supply lines require urgent, localized action.

The National Landscape

How much blood is donated in Malaysia, and which states lead the way?

Donations
Over Time

A closer look at monthly blood donation patterns from 2020 through 2025 shows a system vulnerable to external disruptions and national crisis. While monthly donations fluctuate throughout the year, the overall trajectory points upward, with donation volumes reaching new highs more frequently after 2022.

The absolute lowest point across the timeline occured in April 2020. This dramatic drop was caused by the sudden enforcement of the first Movement Control Order (MCO 1.0), which led to the mass cancellation of mobile donation drives and left citizens hesitant to leave their homes.

Seasonal Rhythms

Following the 2020 shock, the recurring annual drops represent festive, most notably the fasting month of Ramadan, where donations naturally experience a slowdown.

Monthly Blood Donations, 2020 - 2025

Aggregate monthly donations across all states. The historic low in April 2020 highlights the fragility of relying on mobile drives during a strict lockdown, while subsequent troughs map onto Malaysia's annual festive calendar.

Donations per 1,000 Eligible People - State Level

Normalising donation counts by the eligible population isolates regional performance from sheer state size, highlighting massive geographical disparities across Malaysia.

The Capital Corridor Outlier

The data reveals a staggering gulf between the capital and the rest of the nation. Kuala Lumpur leads with an exceptional 865.0 donations per 1,000 eligible people, while the second-highest state, Melaka, sits far behind at 254.9. This massive disparity reflects the extreme centralization of healthcare resources, the presence of the National Blood Centre (PDN) in KL, and data aggregation rules that combine Putrajaya's records with KL.

Blood Type Proportions by State

The percentage share of each blood type collected at the state level. While blood type distributions are largely consistent across Peninsular Malaysia, regional ethnic demographics drive localized variances.

Sabah Demography Deviation

While type AB consistently maintains a small but steady ~5% share across all regions, Sabah stands out as a massive nationwide anomaly. In Sabah, Type O blood accounts for nearly half of all donations (~48%), forcing the proportion of Type B blood down significantly compared to the peninsula. This distinct pattern highlights how East Malaysia's unique indigenous demographics dictate localized blood bank supply profiles.

Annual Donation Trends - State Comparison, 2020 - 2025

Comparing annual donation volumes across Malaysia highlights a striking centralization of the country's blood supply. While most states remain flat, the absolute volume tells a story of an immense regional gulf.

The highlighted line isolates Kuala Lumpur as the primary engine of national collections, while all other states and territories are greyed out at the bottom to illustrate the scale of this disparity.

The Capital Surge

Far from suffering a prolonged pandemic decline, Kuala Lumpur's annual volume surged from roughly 170k in 2020 to over 200k by 2022. This massive increase reflects the consolidation of collections into major central hubs like the National Blood Centre (PDN) to guarantee supply security during turbulent years.

Population,
Age and
Donors

Sheer population size is a poor predictor of blood donation volume. Local age structure and regional healthcare dynamics shape donor availability profoundly.

A stark paradox is exposed: Selangor holds the largest population (~6.5M+) but collects a fraction of the blood managed by Kuala Lumpur, which processes nearly 200k donations annually despite having under 2 million residents.

The Volume Inversion

The chart proves that geography, not the time of year, dominates Malaysia's blood supply data. Each state's row remains almost entirely uniform from January to December. Rather than seasonal shifts, it emphasizes the massive divide between Kuala Lumpur's constant high-volume collections (peaking near 106,309) and the lower, flat baselines of the other states.

Demographic Difference

The proportions vary noticeably across regions. Putrajaya and Sabah command the highest eligible donor shares with adults making up over 90% of their tracked population structure. Conversely, Perak and Perlis show the most advanced aging profiles, leaving them with compressed adult pools and a significantly higher proportion of elderly residents (>60 Years Old)

Where Is Blood Needed?

Hospital infrastructure reveals where demand for donated blood is highest.

Hospital Beds as a Demand Proxy

Blood supply is only meaningful when measured against regional healthcare capacity. Hospital bed density is a strong proxy for medical need.

The critical vulnerability zones are regions where a high density of hospital beds (darker blue) overlaps with a historically low or aging donor base.

Compare Maps

Compare this with the donation rate map. States that display dark blue here (high demand) but were pale orange on previous maps (low collection footprint) run the highest systemic risk of supply-demand friction.

The Perak Vulnerability

Perak stands out as the darkest region on the map, reaching the "Very High" tier of 4.5 or more beds per 1,000 people. Combined with its older population structure, this massive medical infrastructure concentration creates an intense localized reliance on steady blood supply lines.

Hospital Beds per 1,000 People - State Level

Geographic distribution of hospital beds normalized per 1,000 residents. Darker blue regions represent higher relative concentration of medical infrastructure.

Hospital Bed Distribution Across States Over Time

This heatmap shows hospital bed capacity per state over time. Tracking these multi-year shifts clarifies where regional healthcare infrastructures and their corresponding blood demands are concentrating most heavily.

Selangor dominates the volume baseline, having total beds at around 7600 in 2022. Meanwhile, states like Johor, Perak, Sabah, and Kuala Lumpur present a stable, highly concentrated secondary tier of infrastructure.

Expanding Demand

States where bed counts grow fastest require the most attention. Increased hospital capacity without increased donors worsens the crisis.

Bridging the Gap

Combining supply and demand reveals which states need urgent action.

Ranking Supply Against Demand

Plotting donation rate ranks alongside hospital bed ranks exposes the alignment or misalignment between blood supply and demand.

Since peak rankings are placed at the top (Rank 1 = Best), an upward-sloping line identifies a structural vulnerability where a state's healthcare capacity heavily outpaces its internal donation performance.

Needs Attention (Orange)

Orange lines flag regions with supply deficits. Kuala Lumpur displays the most critical upward gap, resting at a modest 9th in donation rank but scaling to 1st in hospital bed concentration. Perak, Negeri Sembilan, Pahang and Kelantan display similar structural imbalances.

Well Supplied (Blue)

Teal lines show states where donations exceed hospital demand. Melaka and Pulau Pinang exhibit steep downward slopes, leading the nation in donation performance (1st and 2nd) while maintaining lower relative bed rankings. These states serve as essential pillars for cross-regional supply redistribution

In Deficit, Priority Targets

Active states extending left (such as Selangor and Kelantan) are major priority targets for targeted donation campaigns, as local donor collection rates do not fully sustain their baseline healthcare infrastructures.

Population Eligibility Breakdown by State

Demographic Shift

As regional populations age, the primary eligible donor core (ages 18 to 60) faces systemic pressure. The dual challenge of a contracting baseline donor pool and rising healthcare utilization among seniors underscores the need for proactive youth recruitment campaigns.
